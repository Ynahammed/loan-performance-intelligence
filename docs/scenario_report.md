# Scenario Report

Generated by `scripts/build_scenario_report.py` from the artifacts the transition engine writes. Every figure is computed; none is transcribed by hand.

> **Simulations under stated assumptions, not forecasts.** The engine applies the multipliers in `macro_scenarios.csv` faithfully. It does not judge whether those assumptions are plausible, and nothing here should be read as a prediction of what will happen.

> **Synthetic data.** The pack was generated to match the challenge specification. No figure describes real borrowers or real portfolio behaviour.

## 1. How a scenario is applied

Scenarios run through the same discrete-time transition model that produces the survival curves — there is no separate scenario model. A scenario is a multiplicative stress on the transition probabilities:

- transitions into a **more severe** delinquency state are scaled by `delinquency_multiplier`
- transitions into **Default** are scaled by `default_multiplier`
- transitions into **Prepaid** are scaled by `prepayment_multiplier`
- **cures** are left alone; stressing them is a separate assumption and is not smuggled in here

The change is then absorbed into the stay-in-state cell so each row still sums to one. Renormalising the whole row instead would dilute the stress back out — a subtle way to make a scenario engine appear to work while doing nothing.

Because Default and Prepaid are both absorbing states in one chain, the probability mass accumulated in each is a true cumulative incidence function: the competition between the two events is handled by construction rather than by treating one as censoring.

| high_prepayment | 1.00% | 30.62% | 1.86% | -0.35 pp |

Under the adverse-credit scenario, projected 12-month default rises from 1.35% to 3.22% — a factor of 2.38. Prepayment falls from 16.04% to 9.38% as the stress suppresses refinancing.

Under the high-prepayment scenario, prepayment rises from 16.04% to 30.62% and default falls to 1.00%: loans that prepay cannot subsequently default, so accelerating prepayment mechanically removes exposure. That interaction comes out of the competing-risk structure rather than being assumed.

## 6. What these projections assume

- **Multipliers are inputs, not estimates.** They come from `macro_scenarios.csv` as supplied. The engine does not derive them from macro data or validate them.
- **Covariates evolve mechanically only.** Loan age advances, remaining term decreases, and the balance follows the contractual amortisation schedule. Credit band, LTV, state and servicer are held at their last observed values — borrower re-underwriting and servicing transfers are not modelled.
- **This synthetic pack contains no cure transitions.** Zero observed moves out of 60DPD or 90DPD back toward current. Real servicing data has substantial cure rates, so these curves are optimistic about severity progression. The state machine can represent cures; this data simply has none.
- **Thin states are estimated from few observations.** Transitions out of 90DPD rest on 34 training rows, so the far end of the ladder is the least reliable part of the chain.
- **Segment projections inherit all of the above**, and thin segments additionally carry sampling noise that is not quantified here.
