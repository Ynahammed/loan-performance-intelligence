## Do the confidence bands hold up?

```
         confidence    n  observed_rate  mean_prediction    brier  roc_auc
    high confidence 5062        0.08633          0.02583 0.057096   0.8081
     low confidence 5062        0.03773          0.00982 0.036619   0.6142
moderate confidence 5061        0.04564          0.00578 0.043301   0.6456

Spearman(absolute spread, mean prediction): +0.987
Spearman(relative spread, mean prediction): +0.236
```

A confidence label that does not track actual accuracy is decoration, so this table is the check -- and it passes only on the metric that is comparable across the bands.

**Read ROC-AUC, not Brier, down this table.** Brier scales with base rate, and these bands have different base rates, so the high-confidence band's worse Brier reflects its higher event rate rather than worse probabilities. On ROC-AUC, which is prevalence-invariant, the ordering holds and the label means something.

**Why the bands use relative spread, with the evidence.** Absolute prediction spread is very nearly a restatement of predicted risk level -- Spearman +0.987 against the mean prediction. Banding on it would produce a 'confidence' label that is really just a risk label wearing a different name. Dividing by the mean prediction removes almost all of that: the rank correlation drops to +0.236. So the normalisation is doing real work, and the bands are not merely risk deciles relabelled.

A residual caution: the high-confidence band still carries roughly 2.3x the event rate of the low-confidence band. At a rank correlation of +0.236 that gap is mostly signal rather than artifact -- models agree with each other where the signal is strong, and that is also where events concentrate -- but the two are not fully separable here, and the label should be read as 'the model is stable on this record' rather than as an independent risk-free guarantee.
